## What Resources Matter in Build and Conquer?

A growing city needs more than money: it also needs workers, power, water, food, and materials for production. Those needs matter because expanding buildings without supporting their inputs can leave your economy unable to support further growth. The [official Build and Conquer experience page](https://www.roblox.com/games/103767961452227/Build-and-Conquer) confirms that players manage money, workers, population, resources, happiness, power, research, and production, but it does not publish a complete resource list or production rates.

## Keep Your City Supplied

Build and Conquer starts you with an empty plot. The official description identifies neighborhoods, farms, shops, factories, power plants, and water networks as parts of a developing city. That suggests a practical way to evaluate each expansion: check what a new building needs, then check whether your city can supply it.

The [City Management page on The Slop Company Wiki](https://the-slop-company.fandom.com/wiki/City_Management) describes these relationships in more detail:

- **Housing** provides citizens and workers, but residential buildings need water and energy.
- **Commercial buildings** generate money and require workers.
- **Farms** provide food for citizens.
- **Power and water buildings** support the buildings that depend on those services.

The wiki’s building lists may not reflect the current beta version, so use them to understand the resource relationships rather than as a guaranteed current build order.

## Connect Raw Materials to Production

According to the City Management wiki page, resource buildings supply **coal, iron, and oil**. It identifies mines and oil pumps as resource buildings, and describes those raw materials as inputs for consumer goods and military production. The same page says industrial buildings produce consumer goods and fuel.

That makes extraction part of a larger chain, not an end goal by itself. Before expanding industry or military manufacturing, check the requirements shown in your current game: you may need raw materials alongside enough money, workers, and utility capacity. The available sources do not establish exact extraction rates, conversion recipes, or the best number of each building.

## Plan Expansion Around Dependencies

The official description ties economic development to research, vehicle and missile manufacturing, and defense. For resource planning, the useful principle is to keep the city functions that support production growing together.

When you add housing, account for its power and water needs. When you add shops or other commercial buildings, account for workers. When you pursue manufacturing, check the relevant material requirements before committing to more production buildings. This is a planning framework based on the documented dependencies, not a claim that one fixed sequence works for every city or update.

## Build and Conquer Resources FAQ

### What are the main Build and Conquer resources?

The official game description names money, workers, population, resources, happiness, and power among the systems players manage. It also includes farms and water networks. The City Management wiki specifically identifies coal, iron, and oil as raw materials.

### How do you get coal, iron, and oil?

The City Management wiki lists **mines** and **oil pumps** as resource buildings that provide those materials. It does not give a clear, current breakdown of each building’s output, so check the in-game building information before planning around a specific yield.

### Do houses need power and water?

The City Management wiki says residential buildings need energy and water to function and provide workers. Check coverage and supply as your neighborhoods expand, especially because building details can change during the game’s beta.
